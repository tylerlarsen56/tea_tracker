Disclaimer

Tea Price Tracker is an amateur tool used to quickly gather (on a weekly recurring basis) and filter thousands of tea products across a dozen online tea merchant websites. It was created to provide easy consumer access to the many unique teas sourced and put up for sale by a handful of incredible tea people, with the goal of helping the consumer find more of what they enjoy as well as explore what they haven’t yet tried. It is my hope this tool creates value for both consumers and business owners alike.

This tool was primarily made through an iterative process which relied on artificial intelligence to write the code, which means mistakes are bound to be made. Additionally, developing a set of keyword filters to accurately tag and place tens of thousands of unique tea products which are constantly updating is inherently challenging and subject to human quirks and errors. If any particular listings are incorrectly filtered, submit your concern with the “Contact” button.

For the purpose of consistency (both within the tool itself and with the Western market that I swim in), I’ve opted to sort Chinese “Red teas” as Black teas, and Chinese “Black Teas” as Puer. I apologize if this makes you, the presumed tea expert reading this disclaimer, throw up in your mouth a little bit.

Lastly, please refer to the amazing, wonderful tea people who run these businesses for the most accurate information about any listing. This tool is mainly here to help get started. Thank you and enjoy the Tea Price Tracker!

Tea types

The types of tea included on the Tea Price Tracker are White, Green, Yellow, Black, Ripe (Shou) Puer, Raw (Sheng) Puer, Purple, Heicha (Dark), Herbal, and Other. See this article by MeiMei Fine Teas for more information on tea types, and see this article by Path of Cha for a comparison between Heicha and Puer

Form factor

Tea comes in many form factors, but it’s generally kept either as loose leaves or pressed together. Loose leaf teas can be steeped easily when put into tea bags, strainers, or other vessels as long as there’s room for the leaves to expand! Pressed teas will either need to be carefully broken into smaller pieces before steeping (most tea cakes, bricks) or just drop it in your tea vessel (minis, dragon balls) for a tea session. Other form factors include flowering teas, which bloom marvelously when steeped, or tea resin, which completely dissolves in water to become rehydrated tea once again.
